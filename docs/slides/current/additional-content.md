## Connect your graphs to drive transformation

This slide shows that one graph can serve many parts of a business. Each circle is a business area. The lines show how the areas connect.

- **Graph:** A graph stores data as things and the links between them. Questions about links get fast answers.
- **Employees:** Graphs map who works where and who knows what. Teams use this for talent development, career management, and org charts.
- **Suppliers:** Graphs show how goods move. Teams use this for route planning, supply chain visibility, inventory planning, and risk analysis.
- **Product:** Graphs link products to buyers and parts. Teams use this for recommendations, new product launches, inventory, and pricing.
- **Customers:** Graphs show what each customer likes and does. Teams use this for loyalty programs, churn prevention, offers, dynamic pricing, and ads.
- **Network and Security:** Graphs link users, devices, and access. Teams use this for identity resolution, reputation scoring, threat detection, and zero trust.
- **Process:** Graphs show how work flows between steps. Teams use this to find bottlenecks, improve processes, and automate them.
- **Transactions:** Graphs follow money between accounts. Teams use this for anti-money laundering, payment scams, credit risk, claims, and fraud.
- **Why connect them:** Each area shares data with the others. A fraud ring in Transactions can link to a stolen identity in Network and Security. One connected graph lets you see both.
- **Takeaway:** Start with one area. Add the next area to the same graph. Each new area makes the earlier ones more useful.

## Graph makes it easy to explore hidden patterns

Graph algorithms are ready-made math routines that run on a graph. They read the links and return answers.

- **Graph algorithm:** A graph algorithm is a routine that scores or groups the things in a graph by how they link.
- **What's important:** Centrality algorithms find the most linked things, such as the busiest account.
- **What's unusual:** Community and similarity algorithms find groups that share details, such as people with the same SSN.
- **What's next:** Path and link prediction algorithms find likely connections, such as the next product a customer wants.
